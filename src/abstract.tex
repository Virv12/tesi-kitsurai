Building multi-tenant key-value stores with predictable and stable performance
turns out to be difficult, due to the need for tackling several performance engineering challenges.
As a result, most of the NoSQL systems offer best-effort throughput and weak isolation under contention.

We present \Kitsurai, an open-source, distributed and leaderless store that provides explicit
per-table throughput reservations while retaining horizontal scalability and high availability.
Reservations are admitted at table creation through a cluster-wide allocator;
enforcement relies on a bandwidth-proportional replication ring that balances
load across availability zones and respects per-node budgets.

Clients tune quorums ($N$, $R$, $W$) to trade latency and durability.
To keep the data path simple and resource-efficient, \Kitsurai avoids
server-side reconciliation and background repair, tolerating multi-value reads
when versions diverge.

\Kitsurai sustains reserved rates under co-located hot tenants, scales close to
analytic expectations across uniform and skewed workloads, and keeps tail
latency modest without explicit latency control mechanism.
By isolating tenants by construction \Kitsurai turns capacity planning into an
explicit, auditable contract, suggesting a practical path to predictable
performance in simple, leaderless key-value architectures.
\Kitsurai is implemented in the Rust programming language for enhanced efficiency and safety,
and the availability of a growing ecosystem of high-quality libraries that simplify the development of complex distributed systems.
