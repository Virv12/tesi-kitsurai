Building multi-tenant key-value stores with predictable and stable performance
turns out to be difficult, due to the need for tackling several performance engineering challenges.
As a result, most of the NoSQL systems offer best-effort throughput and weak isolation under contention.

We present Rustikai, an open-source, distributed and leaderless store providing explicit
per-table throughput reservations, measured in request per second (\unit{\rps}),
while retaining horizontal scalability and high availability.
Reservations are admitted at table creation through a cluster-wide allocator;
enforcement relies on a throughput-proportional replication ring that balances
load across availability zones and respects per-node budgets.
%
Clients can tune per-table quorum configurations to trade latency for durability.
To keep the data path simple and resource-efficient, Rustikai avoids
server-side reconciliation and background repair, tolerating multi-value reads
when versions diverge.

Rustikai sustains reserved throughputs under co-located hot tenants, scales close to
analytic expectations across uniform and skewed workloads, and keeps tail
latency modest without explicit latency control mechanism.
By isolating tenants by construction Rustikai turns capacity planning into an
explicit, auditable contract, suggesting a practical path to predictable
performance in simple, leaderless key-value architectures.
Rustikai is implemented in the Rust programming language, which comes with
a growing ecosystem of high-quality libraries that simplify the development of complex distributed systems,
resulting into enhanced efficiency and safety.
