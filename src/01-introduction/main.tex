\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

In recent years, replicated storage services in the field of \textit{Cloud Computing} have gained significant importance.
In a context where applications must serve millions of users simultaneously, often geographically distributed, it becomes crucial to design systems capable of providing horizontal scalability, high availability, and fault tolerance.

These properties must coexist with the need to keep response times low and ensure efficient resource management.
Traditional data management systems based on relational models are often unsuitable for these new distributed scenarios.
For this reason, modern architectures increasingly rely on \textit{NoSQL} solutions that, in exchange for simpler operations, offer lower overhead and better scalability compared to traditional \textit{SQL} databases.

Another aspect of growing importance is data access latency, as observed on platforms such as \textit{DynamoDB} \cite{Dynamo2007}, which guarantee response times below 10~ms \cite{DynamoWhitepaper}.
However, providing latency and performance guarantees in distributed and \textit{multi-tenant} environments proves extremely challenging.

Major open-source solutions like \textit{MongoDB} \cite{Mongo} or \textit{Cassandra} \cite{Cassandra} adopt a \textit{best effort} approach, without guaranteeing precise limits on latency or throughput, especially when multiple clients share resources.
The \textit{multi-tenant} model, in which several customers share the same physical or virtual resources, introduces additional challenges in workload management and in guaranteeing minimum performance levels.
In the absence of explicit isolation mechanisms, a particularly active \textit{tenant} can compromise the quality of service offered to others, making precise throughput control for each client essential.

The \textit{Kitsurai} project is presented as a first attempt to create a \textit{key-value store} that is not only scalable and resilient, but also introduces explicit resource control mechanisms to guarantee a minimum throughput to each tenant, even under heavy load conditions.

The project was developed in \textit{Rust}, a language chosen not only for its excellent performance but also for the memory safety model it provides.
\textit{Rust} is known for delivering performance comparable to \textit{C++} while avoiding common classes of errors such as \textit{race conditions} or \textit{memory leaks}, making it particularly suitable for programming complex distributed systems.

The following chapters analyze in detail the design choices, the system architecture, and the results obtained through empirical tests, with particular attention to throughput and latency guarantees for clients.
